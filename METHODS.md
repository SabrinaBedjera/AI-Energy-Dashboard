# Methods: Canada data centre dashboard data

Prepared Nov 10–12, 2026. Personal project, views my own.

Rule for anyone building or checking this dashboard: use only the numbers in these files. Do not add, estimate or fill in any number. If a value is "Not disclosed" or blank, show "Not disclosed", never zero.

## 1. projects.csv (51 rows)

**What it is:** named data centre projects in Canada (operating, under construction, proposed), one row per project.

**How rows were found:**

- Alberta: Government of Alberta Major Projects inventory (data centre entries), plus AESO large-load page and company releases.
- Other provinces: company releases, municipal pages and council reports, news, and the York University paper (SSRN 6464099) as a lead list.
- Each row was then checked against its own source where possible. The `check_status` column says how far each row was checked (26 "Confirmed at source"; 6 "From tracker, not yet opened by me", which means the source page could not be opened, so treat these as lower confidence).
- Left out on purpose: Synapse Olds (AB), Île-des-Chênes (MB), Colo-D Saint-Bruno (QC) because they were rejected or never built.

**Columns:**

- `reported_mw`: the MW number exactly as the source states it. Blank = not disclosed.
- `what_the_mw_measures`: MW numbers mean different things (power plant size, IT load, grid allocation). This column says which. Do not call the total "IT capacity".
- `location_precision`: all pins are approximate (city/area level, or the Gov of AB pin which itself says it is not exact). Do not present them as exact sites.
- `source`, `source_2`: links for every row. `date_checked` = 2026-11-10.

**Known judgement calls:**

- Wonder Valley (AB): 1,400 MW = Phase 1 from the Gov of AB listing. The company says up to 7.5 GW for the full build-out. These are phases, not a conflict. Show 1.4 GW with a note.
- AESO's 970 MW large-load allocation is likely the Meta Sturgeon project, so it is not counted twice.
- Cloud regions (AWS, Google, Azure, Oracle): 8 rows, MW not disclosed.

**Totals:** planned (proposed + under construction) with disclosed MW ≈ 10,720 MW. Alberta ≈ 8,160 MW.

## 2. provinces.csv

**What it is:** one row per province with planned MW, share of active sites, main power source, grid rules, clean energy level and openness.

- `planned_mw`, `share_of_active_sites`, `clean_energy_level`, `openness`: from the York University paper (SSRN 6464099, Fig 3b, Section 3, Table 2).
- `grid_rules`: from the grid operator, government, and law firm summaries linked in `grid_rules_sources`.
- `check_status` says what was confirmed.

**Important:** York's provincial totals (≈22,200 MW planned) and our 51-project list (≈10,720 MW) count different things. York includes unnamed sites, and some projects were announced after York's data. Do not compare the two as if they should match, and do not calculate one as a percentage of the other.

## 3. energy_mix_2025.csv

**What it is:** share of electricity generated by source, by province, for full year 2025.

- Source: Statistics Canada, Table 25-10-0015-01 (https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/t1/tbl1/en/tv.action?pid=2510001501).
- Method: monthly generation for all producers, summed Jan to Dec 2025, then each source divided by the total. `Sum of parts %` = 100 for every row. `Months of data` shows coverage.

## 4. power_lines.geojson + power_line_voltage_checks.csv

**Line shapes (base):** NRCan CanVec 1:250K power lines, national file canvec_250K_CA_Res_MGT_shp.zip (https://open.canada.ca/data/en/dataset/92dbea79-f644-4a62-b25e-8eb993ca0264). 3,307 lines, about 71,300 km. Official, but has no voltage. Simplified slightly (about 300 m) so the file loads fast. These are mostly high-voltage transmission lines, not local distribution.

**Voltage (added):** from OpenStreetMap, via Open Infrastructure Map's power layer (downloaded Nov 12, 2026). For each NRCan line, points were sampled every ~2 km and matched to the nearest OpenStreetMap line within 1.5 km.

- A voltage was kept only if at least 40% of the line sat on an OpenStreetMap line, and the voltage was taken from the most common match.
- Strong match = at least 70% of the line matched, and 80% of those points agreed on one voltage.

**Checking the voltage:** for each province, I checked which voltage levels the grid operator or government says are used (power_line_voltage_checks.csv, with links). This confirms that the voltage level exists on that grid. It does not confirm each individual line.

**`voltage_status` column (three values):**

- "Voltage from OpenStreetMap, level confirmed by grid operator": strong match, and that voltage is a confirmed level for that province. About 61% of km.
- "Voltage from OpenStreetMap, not verified": weaker match, or a voltage level I could not confirm from an official source (for example New Brunswick, Nova Scotia, PEI, NWT, and Quebec 161 kV). About 25% of km.
- "No voltage (NRCan line only)": no OpenStreetMap match. About 14% of km.

**How to show it:** colour lines by voltage band (e.g. 450 kV and up, 200–449 kV, under 200 kV, unknown). Show unverified lines with a dashed or lighter style. Say "approximate" in the legend.

## 5. clean-power-stations.geojson

Operating clean power generating stations (biomass, hydro, nuclear, solar, tidal, wind) from Natural Resources Canada's Clean Energy Resources and Projects (CERP) map service, downloaded Nov 12, 2026: https://geoappext.nrcan.gc.ca/arcgis/rest/services/Energy/CERP_EN/MapServer (layers 1–6). 1,078 stations. MW and commissioning year exactly as NRCan lists them. Open Government Licence – Canada.

## 6. drainage-areas.geojson

Major drainage areas from Natural Resources Canada's Atlas of Canada (Standard Drainage Area Classification), from https://geoappext.nrcan.gc.ca/arcgis/rest/services/NRCAN/AtlasWatershedsEN/MapServer (layer 3). Simplified for the web (about 5 km). Shows which river system an area drains into. It does not show water use or water stress. Open Government Licence – Canada.
